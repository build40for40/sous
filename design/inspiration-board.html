What should Hey Sue feel like?

Inspiration for the three screens: pick a recipe, preferences, and cooking. It comes from cooking apps, from voice-first products where you barely see the screen, and from places home cooks never look. Each ticket ends with the one thing we could take from it.

In the kitchen

Cooking apps and what they get right in cook mode

Cooking app01

StepChef

You say "Next step" or "Start timer". It finds the timers in the recipe text and runs them on the lock screen.

TakePull every duration out of the recipe so Sous can offer the timer before you ask.

Audio cookbook03

Olivari Audio Cookbook

A UNIT9 project that turns a cookbook into something you listen to while you cook. It's the closest thing to Sous in spirit.

TakeTreat the recipe as a script that's read aloud. Write it for the ear, not the page.

Reality check04

"Hands-free" usually isn't

A 2021 hands-on test of voice cooking apps found every one still needed a tap on the screen mid-cook. Many 2026 apps claim to be fully hands-free, but most of those claims come from the vendors themselves.

TakeOur bar: from "Hey Sue" to plating, the cook never touches the glass.
Cook
medium.com · 55 Minutes
Cooking app05

YellChef offline mode

Recipes keep working without a connection. Kitchens often have bad Wi-Fi.

TakeDecide what Sous says when the brain can't be reached. "I lost you, the next step is…" is better than silence.
Cook
mwm.ai · YellChef

Products where the screen mostly gets out of the way

AI voice06

ChatGPT voice orb

A breathing orb, like being on a phone call. OpenAI said the aim was for people not to focus on the UI at all. Voice later moved inline into the chat.

TakeThe cook screen is an orb that shows Sous's state: listening, thinking, talking, waiting on you.
Cook

Audio coaching07

Nike Run Club guided runs

One familiar coach's voice in your ear for the whole run. COLLINS gave each run type (Everyday, Speed, Long) its own visual system.

TakeSous is a character, not a reader. Give each recipe type (weeknight, project, bake) its own look.
CookPick
wearecollins.com · Nike Run Club
OS pattern08

Live Activities and the Dynamic Island

Turn-by-turn directions sit small in the island and expand only when a lane change or exit is coming.

TakeThe current step and timers live on the lock screen. They grow only when you need to act.

Voice principle09

Say it, don't show it

Without buttons, people lose their bearings. Good voice systems number their options and suggest what you can say.

TakeNumber the recipe list so the cook can say "Hey Sue, number three". Show one hint of what to say.
PickPrefs
polito.it · conversational agents

Far from the kitchen (mostly)

Interfaces home cooks never see

Restaurant10

Kitchen display system at the pass

Tickets change color as they age (fresh, warning, late) and can be read from across the kitchen. Courses are fired on a delay so the whole order lands at the pass together. A cook bumps a ticket to clear it.

TakeSous times each part of the dish so the rice, sauce and protein finish together. Timers age from green to amber.
Cook
vp0.com · KDS UI
Aviation11

The jet cockpit

Amber Case points out that a cockpit is very complex, but laid out so pilots take it in from the corner of their eye. They only look straight at something when it's wrong.

TakeThe screen stays quiet and ambient. It speaks up only when something needs you, like the pan being about to burn.
Cook
calmtech.institute
Ambient audio12

Endel

A plain black-and-white interface with a slowly changing animation for each soundscape. It's designed to be there without you really noticing it.

TakeEach recipe gets its own slowly drifting visual, so the phone on the counter feels like part of the kitchen.
Cook
apple.com · Endel
Minimal phone13

Light Phone III

Menus are plain text in one color, with no icons and a physical scroll wheel. Reviewers love the calm, though some found it frustratingly sparse.

TakeThe recipe picker could be a plain typographic list instead of a grid of Instagram thumbnails.
Pick
engadget.com · review
Smart home14

mui Lab board

A strip of wood on the wall that looks like furniture until you touch it. Then text and icons appear in the grain.

TakeThis points toward the e-ink or physical device idea from kickoff: Sous as a kitchen object, not an app.

Sous's face, made of food

Alternatives to the AI orb, shown on the black background we've decided on. Each has to show four states: waiting, listening, thinking and talking.

Strong pick

The burner

A ring of blue gas flame, seen from above. It's the most kitchen-native shape there is, and it glows beautifully on black.

Waiting low pilot flameListening flame rises with your voiceThinking flame flickers around the ringTalking flame surges as Sous speaks

The simmer

A pan of sauce from above with bubbles rising and popping. It's warm and alive, and the colour could match the dish you're making.

Waiting the odd lazy bubbleListening gentle simmerThinking rolling boilTalking bubbles pop in time with speech
Strong pick

The ingredients

The recipe's own ingredients as coloured dots: tomato, basil, saffron. Every recipe gets a different face, and a dot fades once that ingredient is used.

Waiting slow driftListening dots lean toward the centreThinking dots spin fasterTalking dots pulse with speech

The yolk

A fried egg whose yolk wobbles. It's playful and instantly reads as food, but it might be too cute for every recipe.

Waiting still and glossyListening yolk leans inThinking slow wobbleTalking jiggles with speech

The steam

Wisps rising from a bowl. It's the calmest option and very Endel, but it says less about which state Sous is in.

Waiting faint wispsListening steam bends toward youThinking curls and swirlsTalking puffs with each word

The plate

A plate seen from above that fills with colour as you go. It's a progress bar more than a face, so it could sit alongside one of the others.

Progress step 4 of 9, plate 44% fullPresent full plate, your cue to plate up
