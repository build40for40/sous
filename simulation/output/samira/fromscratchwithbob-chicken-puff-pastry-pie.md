# Chicken & Puff Pastry Pie

A whole-chicken pie with carrots, celery and onion in a creamy Dijon and herb sauce made with homemade stock, topped with a puff pastry lattice.

From [@fromscratchwithbob](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdJLdRwOkZ3/) · caption

## Ingredients

- [ ] 1 large chicken
- [ ] 4 large carrots, diced
- [ ] 4 sticks celery, diced
- [ ] 1 brown onion, diced
- [ ] 3 heaped tsp Dijon mustard
- [ ] 1 heaped tbsp dried herbs (like tarragon, sage & oregano)
- [ ] 1 heaped tbsp plain flour
- [ ] 800 ml chicken stock (or water)
- [ ] 100 ml cream
- [ ] 1 large handful chopped parsley
- [ ] 250 g puff pastry
- [ ] Egg, for brushing
- [ ] Seasoning, to taste

## Method

1. Slice the breasts off the chicken, cut them into chunks and set aside.
2. Bring a large pan of water to the boil, using just enough to cover the chicken (less water gives a richer stock).
3. Add the rest of the chicken and simmer for 1–2 hours, until the meat falls off the bone. Lift out the chicken and keep the stock.
4. Heat a large ovenproof frying pan or skillet and fry the chicken breast chunks until browned, then remove them.
5. Add the carrots, celery and onion to the pan and cook for 10 minutes until soft. Meanwhile, strip the meat from the boiled chicken.
6. Stir in the dried herbs and mustard, then sprinkle over the flour and stir until it coats the vegetables. Cook for 1 minute.
7. Pour in two thirds of the 800 ml stock and stir until the vegetables are coated in a glossy sauce.
8. Add the browned breast, the stripped meat, the cream and the parsley. Season well.
9. Add more stock as needed until the filling is coated in sauce but not swimming in liquid.
10. Top with a sheet of cold pastry, or cut it into strips for a lattice, working quickly as the hot filling will melt it.
11. Brush with egg and bake at 200°C (180°C fan) for 20–30 minutes, until golden brown.
12. Leave to cool for 10 minutes before serving.

## Tips

- Keep the leftover chicken stock for another meal.
- You may need more or less than 800 ml stock; add only as much as you need for a thick sauce.
- Keep the pastry cold and work fast so the hot filling doesn't melt it.
